# New IT leader 30-60-90 day plan: template

## How to use this template

**Purpose.** A plan for the first three months in an IT leadership role. It structures the time into listening, planning and delivering, so that you learn the business before you change it and show early value without overpromising.

**Guidance**

- Agree the plan with your manager in the first week. Ask what success looks like to them at 30, 60 and 90 days, and write it down.
- Listen first. The first 30 days are for understanding, building relationships and finding out what is not being said.
- Make a small number of visible early wins that matter to the business. Avoid launching major change before you understand the context.
- Keep a running log of what you find (risks, contracts, quirks) and share a short written summary at each milestone.
- Look after the team. Meet each person one to one in the first fortnight and ask the same questions of each.
- Adapt the lists to the size and shape of the organisation. Delete what does not apply.

## Days 1 to 30: listen and learn

**Goal:** understand the business, the technology, the people and the risks.

| Area | Activities | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Stakeholders | Meet executives, department heads and key users. Ask what works, what does not and what they need. | Stakeholder map and themes |
| Team | One to ones with every team member. Review roles, skills and workload. | Team summary |
| Services | Review incident, request and change history for the last 12 months. Attend service desk. | Service baseline |
| Security and resilience | Review the security posture, backup and recovery arrangements, recent incidents and audit findings. | Top security risks |
| Suppliers and contracts | List all contracts, renewal and notice dates, owners, spend. | Contract register |
| Finance | Review budget, actuals, commitments and forecast with finance. | Budget understanding |
| Architecture and assets | Review systems, integrations, licences and asset inventory. | System and asset overview |

**Deliverable at day 30:** a short written summary of findings, risks and quick wins, shared with your manager.

## Days 31 to 60: plan and align

**Goal:** turn findings into a plan the business supports.

| Area | Activities | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Priorities | Rank issues and opportunities by value and risk. Agree the top three priorities with your manager. | Priority list |
| Risk | Create or refresh the risk register. | Risk register |
| Measures | Choose a small set of KPIs and establish baselines. | KPI baseline |
| Reporting | Produce the first board or executive report. | Report |
| Service | Start a monthly service review. Fix one or two visible service irritants. | First review |
| Suppliers | Prepare renewal calendar. Identify contracts to review first. | Renewal calendar |
| Team | Agree development plans and close critical skills gaps. | Team plan |

**Deliverable at day 60:** draft IT strategy outline and 12-month plan.

## Days 61 to 90: deliver and embed

**Goal:** show early results and set the rhythm for the year.

| Area | Activities | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Deliver agreed quick wins. Start the first priority project with a clear plan. | Quick wins delivered |
| Strategy | Present the strategy and roadmap for approval. | Approved direction |
| Budget | Reforecast and flag risks to budget. | Reforecast |
| Governance | Confirm decision rights, change process and reporting rhythm. | Governance in place |
| Communication | Agree how and when IT communicates with the business. | Communications plan |

**Deliverable at day 90:** review with your manager against the agreed success criteria.
