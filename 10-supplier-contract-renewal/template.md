# Supplier contract renewal checklist: template

## How to use this template

**Purpose.** Stop contracts renewing by default. A structured sequence that starts early enough to leave real choices, records the decision and its rationale, and keeps the contract register accurate.

**Guidance**

- Work back from the **notice date**, not the end date. The notice date is the last day you can give notice not to renew, and it is often months before the contract ends. Missing it can commit you to another term.
- The timings below are examples. Start earlier for large, complex or hard-to-replace services.
- Assign a single owner. Record the notice date in a shared calendar with reminders at 180, 120 and 60 days.
- Check the contract itself, not a summary of it. Look for automatic renewal, price indexation, minimum terms, termination rights, data return and exit assistance.
- Get legal and finance input for any contract above your approval threshold.
- Record the decision and the reason, including a decision to renew unchanged.

## 1. Confirm the facts (about 180 days before the notice date)

- [ ] Read the contract and any amendments. Confirm start date, end date, notice period and notice date.
- [ ] Record auto-renewal terms, minimum commitment and price indexation.
- [ ] Record termination rights, service credits and liability limits.
- [ ] Confirm data return, exit assistance and transition obligations.
- [ ] Put the notice date in the shared calendar with reminders at 180, 120 and 60 days.
- [ ] Update the contract register.

## 2. Review value and performance (about 150 days before)

- [ ] Compare cost with budget for the last 12 months.
- [ ] Review SLA performance, incidents and service credits claimed.
- [ ] Review usage: licences, volumes, seats, features actually used.
- [ ] Gather user and stakeholder feedback.
- [ ] List what the service does that would be hard to replace.

## 3. Confirm the requirement (about 120 days before)

- [ ] Is the need still there? Has it changed? Could another service already owned cover it?
- [ ] Define the requirements for the next term, including security, integration and service levels.
- [ ] Check the strategy and roadmap for any plan that affects this service.

## 4. Test the market (about 120 days before)

- [ ] Decide whether to re-tender, benchmark quotes, or negotiate with the incumbent. Record why.
- [ ] If re-tendering, agree criteria and weights first (see the [vendor scorecard](../04-vendor-scorecard/)).
- [ ] Estimate cost and time to switch, including transition risk.

## 5. Risk and compliance (about 90 days before)

- [ ] Review the supplier's security and data protection position and any sub-processors.
- [ ] Check financial health of the supplier.
- [ ] Confirm business continuity and exit plans.
- [ ] Add or update entries in the [risk register](../08-risk-register/).

## 6. Negotiate (about 90 to 60 days before)

- [ ] Agree what you want: price, term, break clause, service levels, service credits, indexation cap.
- [ ] Ask for improvements in exchange for a longer term, not just a lower price.
- [ ] Keep a credible alternative open until the contract is signed.

## 7. Decide and approve (before the notice date, with margin)

- [ ] Write the recommendation, using the [decision memo template](../07-technology-decision-memo/) for significant contracts.
- [ ] Obtain approval at the right level.
- [ ] If not renewing, give written notice in the form and to the address the contract requires, and keep proof of delivery.

## 8. Close out

- [ ] Sign and file the contract. Raise the purchase order.
- [ ] Update the contract register, calendar, budget and risk register.
- [ ] Schedule a performance review at the mid-point of the new term.
- [ ] Record lessons for next time.
